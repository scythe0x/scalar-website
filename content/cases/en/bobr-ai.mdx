## The real problem behind AI presentations

Generating text for ten slides is relatively easy. Generating a coherent presentation is not. The system has to understand narrative structure, visual rhythm, the amount of content, appropriate imagery, and the constraints of PowerPoint and PDF output.

BOBR AI was built to solve that larger problem rather than place generated text into a fixed template.

## Defining the MVP

As lead developer, I was responsible for frontend delivery and coordinated a backend developer and designer. The first objective was a narrow end-to-end path: a user provides a topic, the product creates a deck, and the result can be reviewed and exported.

The MVP was working within one week. That speed was useful because it exposed the difficult parts early instead of hiding them behind months of speculative architecture.

## Frontend as an orchestration layer

The interface had to represent a long-running, multi-step process without making the product feel stuck or unpredictable. It needed clear generation states, recoverable errors, progressive results, and a useful final presentation view.

This made the frontend an orchestration layer between the user, generation pipeline, assets, and export—not a static shell around an API.

## Prompt engineering and visual quality

The most time-consuming problem was teaching the generation system to make decisions that felt visually intentional. We iterated on prompts, content structure, layout logic, and image selection, including image discovery through Yandex search.

<Callout label="Speed versus quality">
A one-week MVP proved the workflow. The following work made the output useful. Fast validation and long quality iteration were both necessary.
</Callout>

## Leading across disciplines

I translated product goals into implementation priorities, aligned frontend work with backend constraints, and kept design decisions connected to what the generation system could reliably produce.

The project required switching between code, product scope, prompt experiments, visual evaluation, and team coordination.

## Result

BOBR AI moved from concept to MVP in one week and reached a full market release in two months. The product continued to evolve after my involvement; this case describes the system and my contribution during that period.
