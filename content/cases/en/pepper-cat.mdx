## Designing for release frequency

PEPPER CAT and the emerging Blessed Music label worked with many artists and a high-frequency release model. Treating every release as a completely independent identity made **production slow and difficult to repeat**.

The system needed to **preserve variation** while making delivery predictable.

## The label became the system

Instead of building every visual package only around an individual artist, **the production language was anchored to the label**. This made the work feel consistent and helped communicate professionalism to new artists entering the ecosystem.

<Callout label="Key decision">
The identity had to support high-frequency production, not fight it.
</Callout>

## A three-stage pipeline

<ProcessSteps>

### 01 — Cover artwork

The reusable structure was reduced to three main layers: background, logo, and foreground text. Removing unnecessary decoration concentrated creative effort on strong backgrounds and art direction.

### 02 — Motion design

Because the layer structure stayed stable, assets could plug into pre-built animation patterns. A new release no longer required rebuilding motion from zero.

### 03 — Export

The final animation was adapted into the formats needed for YouTube, social posts, stories, and other channels.

</ProcessSteps>

## Result

The pipeline delivered **more than 30 releases**. Full-package turnaround was reduced to **under two hours** and revisions to under one hour. Supplied campaign results included a threefold increase in story link clicks, more than 25% growth in music streams, and more than 10% growth in YouTube watch time.
