## One interface, many operational contexts

Support operators often work across disconnected tools: one for chats, another for tasks, a schedule elsewhere, telephony in a separate window, and performance or payout information in yet another system.

InHelp joined those contexts into a role-based workspace.

## Mapping the work before designing screens

The interface had to match a real working day. An operator needed to see what required attention now, understand the status of an application, communicate with a client, and know what came next in the schedule.

The frontend covered:

- customer chats and support requests;
- assigned tasks and processing statuses;
- internal telephony and consultation booking;
- shift planning and workload by time slot;
- earnings, accruals, and payout history;
- performance ratings and feedback;
- profile and payment details.

## Designing for roles and states

An internal product becomes difficult when every role sees the same controls. The interface needed to reveal the right information and actions for each operational context without creating separate disconnected applications.

Scheduling introduced another dimension: team direction, time slots, shift availability, and workload had to remain understandable at a glance.

## Product result

InHelp replaced several fragmented operational surfaces with a single workspace. Operators could see current work, schedule, quality indicators, and financial results, while managers gained a clearer view of team workload.

The case demonstrates frontend work where information architecture and operational clarity matter as much as visual polish.
